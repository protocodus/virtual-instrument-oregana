# Organ registers by national school

Research for Oregana's stop list, 2026-11-12: the registers of reference organs of seven schools between
about 1600 and 1900, with each stop list's source. `Source/DSP/Stops.h` is the stop list built from it.

Historic organs share one core, a chorus of principal pipes from 8' up to 2' and stopped flutes, and each country built something different on top of it. This is a survey of the registers in reference organs of seven schools between about 1600 and 1900, written to choose the stop list of Oregana, the modelled church organ.

- **North Germany** built several complete organs in one case with a pedal from 32' to 2': the organ of the toccata.
- **Central Germany**, Bach's home, added weight at 16' and string, flute and beating colours at 8'.
- **Classical France** fixed two full sounds, the Plein jeu of mixtures and the Grand jeu of reeds and Cornets, and solo colours made of thirds.
- **Symphonic France** turned the organ into an orchestra with harmonic flutes, strings, strong reeds and a swell box.
- **Italy** kept one gentle chorus drawn rank by rank, and a beating Voce umana.
- **Spain** cut every stop in two at middle C and laid its trumpets horizontally.
- **England** stayed small and pedal-less until 1800, invented the swell box, and ended with the Tuba.

For Oregana: the German baroque list of 39 stops now being built covers Bach and is all within the pipe model as it stands. A third enclosed manual with French, Italian and Romantic colours is the open choice. The last two sections give the comparison and the detail.

## The pipe families every school draws on

Every register in every school is one of six kinds of pipe, and the schools differ in which kinds they favour, how they group them and what they call them. A register's number is the length of its lowest pipe: 8' sounds at written pitch, 16' and 32' one and two octaves below, 4', 2' and 1' above. Fractions are mutations, which sound a harmonic other than an octave: 2 2/3' is the twelfth, 1 3/5' the seventeenth (a pure major third), 1 1/3' the nineteenth.

| Family | How the pipe is built | Sound | Names by country |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Principals | Open cylindrical metal flue pipes of medium width | The organ's own tone, neither flute nor string; the backbone of every chorus | Principal, Octave, Superoctave (DE); Montre, Prestant, Doublette (FR); Principale, Ottava, Decimaquinta (IT); Flautado, Octava, Quincena (ES); Open Diapason, Principal, Fifteenth (EN) |
| Flutes | Wide open pipes; stopped pipes (half length, odd harmonics); stopped with a chimney; tapered; double-length overblown | Round and soft, from hollow (stopped) to bright (tapered) | Hohlflöte, Gedackt, Rohrflöte, Spitzflöte, Quintadena (DE); Bourdon, Flûte à cheminée, Flûte harmonique (FR); Flauto in ottava, Flauto in duodecima (IT); Violón, Tapadillo (ES); Stopped Diapason (EN) |
| Strings | Very narrow open pipes, slow to speak | Thin, keen, rich in high harmonics; often a second rank tuned sharp so the pair beats | Viola da Gamba, Salicional, Schwebung (DE); Gambe, Voix céleste (FR); Voce umana, Fiffaro (IT, a principal tuned to beat); Dulciana (EN) |
| Mutations | Single flute or principal ranks at a fifth or third above an octave | Colour a solo line or reinforce a harmonic; never used alone | Quinte, Nasat, Terz, Sesquialtera (DE); Nazard, Tierce, Larigot, Cornet (FR); Nasardo, Corneta (ES); Twelfth, Tierce, Cornet (EN) |
| Mixtures | Three to eight small principal pipes on every key, at octaves and fifths, breaking back to lower pitches as the keys rise | The brilliance on top of the full chorus | Mixtur, Scharff, Zimbel (DE); Fourniture, Cymbale (FR); the separate ripieno ranks (IT); Lleno, Címbala (ES); Mixture, Furniture (EN) |
| Reeds | A brass tongue beating against a shallot, with a resonator: full-length cone, half-length cylinder, or very short | From blazing (cones) through nasal (cylinders) to buzzing (short) | Trompete, Posaune, Krummhorn, Dulzian, Regal, Vox humana (DE); Trompette, Bombarde, Clairon, Cromorne, Hautbois (FR); Tromboncini (IT); Trompeta real, Clarín, Dulzaina (ES); Trumpet, Cremona, Tuba (EN) |

Two compound registers recur everywhere and are worth knowing by name. The **Sesquialtera** is two ranks, 2 2/3' and 1 3/5', a reedy solo colour. The **Cornet** is five wide ranks (8', 4', 2 2/3', 2', 1 3/5') from middle C upward, a full solo voice that also thickens the treble of the reeds.

## North Germany and the Netherlands

The North German organ of about 1650 to 1720 is several complete organs in one case, each division with its own principal chorus, mixture and reeds, and a pedal as large as any manual. This is the instrument of Buxtehude and the young Bach, and the one a toccata was written for.

The reference is Arp Schnitger's organ in [St. Jacobi, Hamburg](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnitger_organ_(Hamburg)>) (1693, restored 1993): 60 stops on four manuals and pedal, about 4,000 pipes, 80 mm wind, with 15 reeds. Bach applied for the organist's post there in 1720.

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Werck (main) | Principal 16', Quintadehn 16', Octava 8', Spitzflöht 8', Viola di Gamba 8', Octava 4', Rohrflöht 4', Super Octav 2', Flachflöht 2', Rauschpfeiff II, Mixtur VI–VIII, Trommet 16' |
| Rückpositiv | Principal 8', Gedackt 8', Quintadehna 8', Octava 4', Blockflöht 4', Octava 2', Querpfeiff 2', Siffloit 1 1/2', Sexquialtera II, Scharff VI–VIII, Dulcian 16', Bahrpfeiffe 8', Trommet 8' |
| Oberpositiv | Principal 8', Rohrflöht 8', Holtzflöht 8', Octava 4', Spitzflöht 4', Nasat 3', Octava 2', Gemshorn 2', Scharff IV–VI, Cimbel III, Trommet 8', Vox humana 8', Trommet 4' |
| Brustpositiv | Principal 8', Octav 4', Hollflöth 4', Waldtflöht 2', Sexquialtera II, Scharff IV–VI, Dulcian 8', Trechter Regal 8' |
| Pedal | Principal 32', Octava 16', Subbaß 16', Octava 8', Octava 4', Nachthorn 2', Rauschpfeiff III, Mixtur VI–VIII, Posaune 32', Posaune 16', Dulcian 16', Trommet 8', Trommet 4', Cornet 2' |

What marks the school:

- **A principal chorus on every division**, each an octave apart at its base (16' in the Werck, 8' in the positives), so the divisions answer each other at different heights and distances.
- **Very large mixtures**, six to eight ranks, and a Rauschpfeife (2 2/3' + 2') beneath them.
- **An independent pedal from 32' to 2'**, with its own mixture and a reed at every pitch. The 2' Cornet reed and the 4' Trommet carry a chorale melody in the feet.
- **Short-resonator reeds** for colour: Dulcian, Regal, Bärpfeife, Vox humana.
- **Flutes in every shape** (stopped, chimney, tapered, wide open), mostly at 8', 4' and 2'.
- Two tremulants, a Zimbelstern (a turning star with bells) and a drum.

Dutch organs of the same period, such as Christian Müller's at St. Bavo, Haarlem (1738), follow the same plan.

## Central and South Germany

The organs of Saxony and Thuringia, which Bach played and examined all his working life, keep the North German chorus but trade its many reeds for weight at 16' and for a wide palette of 8' colours: strings, flutes and beating stops.

The closest thing to a Bach organ is Zacharias Hildebrandt's in [St. Wenzel, Naumburg](https://greifenberger-institut.de/en/wissenswertes/orgel/mitteldeutschland/naumburg_text.php) (1743–1746), 53 stops on three manuals. Bach and Gottfried Silbermann examined it together in September 1746 and passed it. Whether Bach helped to design it is not certain.

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Hauptwerk | Principal 16', Quintathen 16', Octav 8', Spitzflöt 8', Gedackt 8', Octav 4', Spitzflöt 4', Quinta 2 2/3', Octav 2', Weit Pfeiffe 2', Sexquintaltra II, Cornett IV, Mixtur VIII, Bombart 16', Trompet 8' |
| Oberwerk | Burdun 16', Principal 8', Hollflött 8', Unda maris 8', Praestant 4', Gemshorn 4', Quinta 2 2/3', Octav 2', Waldflött 2', Tertia 1 3/5', Quinta 1 1/3', Süfflött 1', Scharff V, Vox humana 8' |
| Rückpositiv | Principal 8', Violdigamba 8', Rohrflött 8', Quintathen 8', Praestant 4', Fugarra 4', Rohrflött 4', Naßat 2 2/3', Octav 2', Rausch Pfeiffe II, Cymbel V, Fagott 16' |
| Pedal | Prinzipal 16', Violon 16', Subbaß 16', Oktav 8', Violon 8', Octav 4', Nachthorn 2', Mixtur VII, Posaune 32', Posaune 16', Trompett 8', Clarin 4' |

Two neighbours show the range of the school:

- **Gottfried Silbermann, [Freiberg Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiberg_Cathedral) (1711–1714)**, 44 stops. Silbermann had trained in Alsace, and his Hauptwerk carries a French five-rank Cornet beside the German Mixtur and Zimbel, with Trompete 8' and Clarin 4'. His reeds are few and strong, his principals bright. The Oberwerk has a Krummhorn and a Vox humana.
- **Tobias Heinrich Gottfried Trost, [Altenburg castle church](https://greifenberger-institut.de/en/wissenswertes/orgel/mitteldeutschland/altenburg_text.php) (1735–1739)**, which Bach played in 1739. Its two manuals hold seven or eight different 8' and 16' colours each: Flaute travers 16', Viol di Gamba, Spitzflöte, Bordun, Rohrflöte, Geigenprincipal, Vagarre (a string), Hohlflöte, Lieblich Gedackt, a two-rank Flaute douce, and a Glockenspiel. The pedal has Posaune 32' and 16'.

What marks the school:

- **Gravity**: a 16' Principal or Bordun on the main manual, 32' Posaune in the pedal.
- **Strings and narrow stops** that the north barely knew: Viola da Gamba, Violon, Fugara, Salicional.
- **Beating stops**: Unda maris, and Silbermann's Schwebung.
- **Thirds in the chorus**: Sesquialtera, Tertia, Cornet, so the plenum is reedier than in the north even without reeds.
- **Fewer and simpler reeds**: Trompete, Posaune, Fagott, Vox humana.

Further south, in Catholic Swabia and Bavaria, builders such as Joseph Gabler ([Weingarten](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_of_the_Basilica_of_St._Martin_(Weingarten)>), 1737–1750) and Karl Joseph Riepp pushed colour furthest: many soft 8' stops, doubled ranks, very large mixtures, a carillon of bells, and few reeds.

## Classical France

The French organ of 1650 to 1790 is the most standardised of all: every organ had the same registers, and composers named the registration in the title of the piece. It is built around two fixed full sounds that are never mixed, the Plein jeu and the Grand jeu.

The reference is François-Henri Clicquot's organ in [Poitiers Cathedral](https://greifenberger-institut.de/en/wissenswertes/orgel/frankreich/poitiers_text.php) (1787–1791), which the Greifenberger Institut calls the last word of the style.

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Grand Orgue | Montre 16', Bourdon 16', Montre 8', Second huit pieds 8', Bourdon 8', Prestant 4', Grande Tierce 3 1/5', Nazard 2 2/3', Doublette 2', Quarte de Nazard 2', Tierce 1 3/5', Grand Cornet V, Fourniture V, Cymbale IV, two Trompettes 8', two Clairons 4', Voix humaine 8' |
| Positif | Montre 8', Bourdon 8', Dessus de flûte 8', Prestant 4', Nazard 2 2/3', Doublette 2', Tierce 1 3/5', Cornet V, Plein-jeu VII, Trompette 8', Cromorne 8', Clairon 4' |
| Récit (treble only) | Flûte 8', Cornet V, Trompette 8', Hautbois 8' |
| Écho (treble only) | Bourdon 8', Flûte 8', Trompette 8' |
| Pédale | Flûte 16', 8', 4', Bombarde 16', Trompette 8', Clairon 4' |

The registrations these stops exist for:

| Registration | What is drawn | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plein jeu | Montres, Bourdons, Prestant, Doublette, Fourniture and Cymbale; no reeds, no thirds | Broad, shimmering, slow harmony |
| Grand jeu | Trompettes, Clairons, Cromorne, Cornets, Prestant; no mixtures | Blazing reeds, the treble filled out by the Cornet |
| Jeu de tierce | Bourdon 8', Prestant, Nazard, Doublette, Tierce | A reedy solo voice made only of flues |
| Tierce en taille | The jeu de tierce as a tenor solo against soft 8' stops | The school's signature slow movement |
| Cromorne, Voix humaine, Trompette | One reed as a solo, or two in dialogue | Named in the titles of the pieces |
| Fonds | All 8' flues together | Warm, the seed of the later symphonic sound |

What marks the school: the wide-scale **Cornet** and separate **tierce** ranks on every manual, reeds built for brilliance in the bass (the Cornet makes up their weak treble), short solo manuals for the right hand only, and a pedal that is only flutes and reeds, used for a slow tune in the tenor or a reed bass.

## Symphonic France

Aristide Cavaillé-Coll (1811–1899) rebuilt the French organ as an orchestra: a smooth crescendo from one soft 8' stop to full organ, made of many 8' foundation stops, harmonic flutes, strings, powerful reeds and an expressive enclosed division. Franck, Widor and Vierne wrote for it.

The reference is his organ in [Saint-Sulpice, Paris](https://www.aross.fr/en/the-great-organ/great-organ-specification/) (1862), about 100 stops on five manuals, built around a Clicquot organ of 1781.

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Grand-chœur | Salicional 8', Octave 4', Fourniture IV, Plein jeu IV, Cymbale VI, Cornet V, Bombarde 16', Basson 16', two Trompettes 8', Basson 8', Clairon 4', Clairon-doublette 2' |
| Grand-orgue | Principal harmonique 16', Montre 16', Bourdon 16', Flûte conique 16', Montre 8', Diapason 8', Bourdon 8', Flûte harmonique 8', Flûte traversière 8', Flûte à pavillon 8', Quinte 5 1/3', Prestant 4', Doublette 2' |
| Positif | Violonbasse 16', Quintaton 16', Salicional 8', Viole de gambe 8', Unda maris 8', Quintaton 8', Flûte traversière 8', Flûte douce 4', Flûte octaviante 4', Dulciane 4', Quinte 2 2/3', Doublette 2', Tierce 1 3/5', Larigot 1 1/3', Picolo 1', Plein-jeu harmonique III–VI, Basson 16', Baryton 8', Trompette 8', Clairon 4' |
| Récit expressif (enclosed) | Quintaton 16', Diapason 8', Bourdon 8', Violoncelle 8', Voix céleste 8', Flûte harmonique 8', Prestant 4', Flûte octaviante 4', Dulciane 4', Nazard 2 2/3', Doublette 2', Octavin 2', Fourniture V, Cymbale IV, Cornet V, Bombarde 16', Trompette 8', Clairon 4', Basson-hautbois 8', Cromorne 8', Voix humaine 8' |
| Solo | Bourdon 16', Flûte conique 16', Principal 8', Bourdon 8', Flûte harmonique 8', Violoncelle 8', Gambe 8', Kéraulophone 8', Prestant 4', Octave 4', Flûte octaviante 4', Octavin 2', Quinte 5 1/3', Tierce 3 1/5', Septième 2 2/7', Quinte 2 2/3', Cornet V, Bombarde 16', Trompette 8', Trompette coudée 8', Clairon 4' |
| Pédale | Principal 32', Principal 16', Contrebasse 16', Soubasse 16', Principal 8', Violoncelle 8', Flûte 8', Flûte 4', Bombarde 32', Bombarde 16', Basson 16', Trompette 8', Ophicléide 8', Clairon 4' |

What is new against the classical organ:

- **Harmonic flutes** (Flûte harmonique, Flûte octaviante, Octavin): pipes of double length with a small hole at the middle, blown to sound their octave. They are full and strong where an ordinary flute thins out in the treble.
- **Strings and a celeste**: Gambe, Violoncelle, Salicional, and the Voix céleste tuned sharp to beat.
- **Many 8' foundations on every manual**, drawn together as the *fonds*.
- **Harmonic and high-pressure reeds**: Trompette, Clairon and Bombarde that stay strong to the top, the reeds of each division brought on by a pedal. At some churches the trumpets lie horizontally (en chamade).
- **The Récit expressif**, enclosed in a box with shutters. The [swell box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swell_box) was English, first advertised in 1712; Cavaillé-Coll gave it a spring-loaded and later a balanced pedal.
- A machine for hail and a nightingale survive from the older taste.

## Italy

The Italian organ keeps one manual, low wind pressure and a single principal chorus whose every rank is its own register, so the player builds the full sound, the ripieno, one harmonic at a time. There is no mixture stop and, until the late 18th century, almost no reeds.

The reference for the early style is the Antegnati organ of [San Carlo, Brescia](https://www.sonusparadisi.cz/en/organs/italy/brescia-st-carlo.html) (the case is dated 1636):

| Group | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Ripieno | Principale 8' (divided bass and treble), Ottava 4', Quintadecima 2', Decimanona 1 1/3', Vigesimaseconda 1', Vigesimasesta 2/3', Vigesimanona 1/2' |
| Flutes | Flauto in ottava 4', Flauto in duodecima 2 2/3' |
| Beating stop | Fiffaro 8' (treble), a second principal tuned sharp, drawn only with the Principale |
| Pedal | Contrabassi 16', pulled down from the manual, added later |

The ranks are named by their interval above the Principale: fifteenth, nineteenth, twenty-second and so on. They are not made very small. Each breaks back an octave when its pipes reach about 1/6' to 1/8', so the top of the keyboard piles up at 2' and 2 2/3'. The source calls the result golden where the German plenum is silver. Costanzo Antegnati's *L'Arte organica* (1608) gives the registrations.

By the time of the Serassi family the same core carried a second layer of colour. Their organ in [Sant'Alessandro in Colonna, Bergamo](https://greifenberger-institut.de/en/wissenswertes/orgel/italien/bergamo_text.php) (1778–1781) adds to the ripieno:

- **Voce umana 8'**, the beating principal under its later name.
- **Cornetto** and **Sesquialtera**, compound stops with a third, taken from France.
- **Strings**: Viola 4' in the bass, Violoncello 8'.
- **Reeds**: Clarino 8', Fagotto 8' in the bass with Trombe 8' in the treble, Bombarde 16' in the pedal.
- **Divided stops** (bassi and soprani, split between b and middle C), so one hand accompanies the other.
- A **Tiratutti**, a lever that draws the whole ripieno at once.

What marks the school: a singing, vocal principal on gentle wind, the ripieno drawn rank by rank, the Voce umana for the Elevation, flutes at 4' and 2 2/3' as the only early solo colours, and a pedal that is only a 16' bass.

## Spain and Portugal

The Iberian organ is usually one manual whose every stop is cut in two at middle C, so the two hands play different sounds, and its trumpets stick horizontally out of the case. It has three choruses that are used apart: principals, wide flutes with thirds, and reeds.

A plain Castilian example is Tadeo Ortega's organ of [1776](https://www.organartmedia.com/en/tadeo-ortega):

| Half | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Bass (left hand) | Flautado 8', Octava 4', Docena 2 2/3', Quincena 2', Decinovena 1 1/3', Lleno, Címbala, Trompeta real 8', Bajoncillo 4', Chirimía 2' |
| Treble (right hand) | Flautado 8', Octava 4', Docena 2 2/3', Quincena and Decinovena, Lleno, Címbala, Corneta, Clarín 8', Oboe 8', Trompeta magna 16' |
| Toys | Bells (Cascabeles), drum (Tambor), bagpipe drone (Gaita) |

The source page prints the two halves in a garbled table, so the assignment of Corneta to the treble follows the usual practice, not the page. The pedal is five notes coupled to the manual.

The school's vocabulary, from The Diapason's survey of [early Iberian organ design](https://www.thediapason.com/content/early-iberian-organ-design-and-disposition):

- **Divided registers** (medio registro), used systematically from the 1560s. They gave rise to the *tiento de medio registro*, a solo in one hand over an accompaniment in the other.
- **The Flautado chorus**: Flautado de 13 palmos (8') in Castile or de 26 (16') in Catalonia, then Octava, Docena, Quincena, Decinovena, Veintidosena, crowned by the Lleno, Címbala and Sobrecímbala mixtures.
- **The Nasardos**, a second chorus of wide flutes at 4', 2 2/3' and 1 3/5', and the **Corneta**, the usual stop to be shut in an echo box whose lid a foot or knee opens. Writers of the time described the effect as distance, not loudness.
- **The reeds**: a Trompeta real 8' standing inside the case, and outside it the horizontal **Trompetas de batalla**, Clarines, Bajoncillo 4', Chirimía, Dulzainas and Orlos (regals). The bass half carries high-pitched reeds and the treble half low ones, up to a Trompeta imperial 32', so the full reed sound is equally dense across the keyboard. Cathedrals mounted them on both fronts for echoes.
- **The pedal** never passed one octave: pull-downs, or a few 16' pipes called contras.

## England

The English organ started again from nothing in 1660, stayed small and mild for 150 years, had no pedal, and then in the 19th century became the home of the swell box and the loudest reeds in Europe.

**1660 to about 1800.** The Puritan ban had destroyed most instruments. The [Greifenberger Institut's survey](https://greifenberger-institut.de/en/wissensvermittlung/orgel/england/einleitung_england.php) names the two founders, Bernard "Father" Smith from near Halle and Renatus Harris, born in France, whose 1684 contest at the Temple Church is the Battle of the Organs. Their stop list barely varied:

| Division | Typical stops |
| --- | --- |
| Great | Open Diapason 8', Stopped Diapason 8', Principal 4', Twelfth 2 2/3', Fifteenth 2', Sesquialtera, Cornet (treble), Trumpet 8' |
| Choir | Stopped Diapason 8', Principal 4', Flute 4', Fifteenth 2', Cremona 8' (from the French Cromorne) |
| Echo, later Swell (treble only) | Open and Stopped Diapason, Principal, Cornet, Trumpet |
| Pedal | None, even on three-manual organs; the manuals often ran down to low F instead |

The Echo's box gained shutters worked by a pedal at St Magnus the Martyr, London, advertised in [1712](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swell_box) as able to swell notes, "which never was in any organ before".

**The Romantic cathedral organ.** Henry "Father" Willis's organ at [Lincoln Cathedral](https://lincolncathedral.com/?p=47517) shows the mature type:

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Great | Double Open Diapason 16', three Open Diapasons 8', Stopped Diapason 8', Claribel Flute 8', Principal 4', Flute harmonique 4', Twelfth, Fifteenth, Mixture III, Trombone 16', Tromba 8', Clarion 4' |
| Swell (enclosed) | Double Open Diapason 16', two Open Diapasons 8', Lieblich Gedeckt 8', Salicional 8', Vox angelica 8', Principal 4', Lieblich Flöte 4', Fifteenth 2', Mixture III, Double Trumpet 16', Trumpet 8', Oboe 8', Vox humana 8', Clarion 4' |
| Choir | Lieblich Bourdon 16', Lieblich Gedeckt 8', Dulciana 8', Viola da Gamba 8', Hohl Flöte 8', Gemshorn 4', Concert Flute 4', Piccolo harmonique 2', Cor anglais 16', Corno di bassetto 8' |
| Solo | Claribel Flute 8', Gamba 8', Voix céleste 8', Harmonic Flute 4', Orchestral Oboe 8', Orchestral Clarinet 8', Tuba 8', Tuba Clarion 4' |
| Pedal | Double Open Diapason 32', Open Wood 16', Open Metal 16', Violone 16', Bourdon 16', Octave 8', Violoncello 8', Super Octave 4', Contra Posaune 32', Ophicleide 16', Clarion 8' |

The table leaves out the stops the cathedral's page dates to 1960. What marks the school: several Open Diapasons of different strengths on one manual, a complete enclosed Swell with its own reed chorus at 16', 8' and 4' (the "full swell" behind closed shutters), orchestral solo reeds, mixtures with a third in them, and the unenclosed **Tuba**, a trumpet on high wind that carries over the whole organ. At [Truro](https://www.trurocathedral.org.uk/music/father-willis-organ) (1887) the cathedral notes that a Willis Tuba is meant to balance the pedal reed in power.

## Romantic Germany

German builders of the 19th century kept the baroque divisions and filled them with soft 8' and 16' stops in fine gradations, so the organ swells by adding stops, not by opening a box. Liszt and Reubke wrote for the first of them.

The reference is Friedrich Ladegast's organ in [Merseburg Cathedral](https://musiqueorguequebec.ca/orgues/allemagne/merseburgsjbsl.html) (1853–1855), 81 stops on four manuals, the largest in Germany when it was finished. Reubke's Sonata was first played on it in 1857. The source prints the manual divisions run together, so the stops are grouped here by kind.

| Kind | Stops on the four manuals |
| --- | --- |
| Principals | Principal 16', 8', 4', Geigenprincipal 8', Octave 4' and 2', Doublette, Quinte 5 1/3' and 2 2/3', Terz 1 3/5' |
| Flutes, open and stopped | Bordun 32' and 16', Lieblich Gedackt 16' and 8', Doppelgedeckt 8', Rohrflöte 8' and 4', Hohlflöte 8', Gemshorn 8' and 4', Flauto traverso 8', Flauto amabile 8', Flauto dolce 8', Zartflöte 4', Spitzflöte 4', Siffleflöte 1', Quintatön 16' and 8' |
| Strings and beating stops | Gambe 8' (two), Salicional 8' and 4', Fugara 8', Unda maris 8' |
| Compound stops | Mixtur IV (three), Scharff IV, Cymbel III, Cornett III–V and II–V, Progressio harmonica II–IV |
| Reeds | Trompete 8', Schalmey 8', and four free reeds: Fagott 16', Aeoline 16', Oboe 8', with Dulcian 16' in the pedal |
| Percussion | Stahlspiel (steel bars) |

The pedal has 20 stops: Untersatz 32', Principal, Salicetbass, Subbass and Violonbass 16', Grossnassat 10 2/3', three 8' stops, Terz 6 2/3', Rohrquinte 5 1/3', three 4' stops, Mixtur IV, Cornett IV, Posaune 32' and 16', Dulcian 16', Trompete 8'.

What marks the school:

- **Many graded 8' stops** of every family, from Aeoline and Flauto dolce up to the Principal, for a seamless crescendo.
- **Free reeds** (the tongue swings through its slot as in a harmonium), softer and rounder than beating reeds: Fagott, Oboe, Aeoline, Clarinette.
- **Low mutations** (10 2/3', 6 2/3', 5 1/3') that thicken the bass and suggest a 32'.
- **A manual 32'**, strings at 16' in the pedal, and a mixture that grows from two to four ranks as it rises (Progressio harmonica).
- Few reeds for the size: two chorus reeds on four manuals. Power comes from the flue stops.

## The schools side by side

Every school has a principal chorus and stopped flutes; almost everything else is a national choice. Each cell says how the register type appears in the reference organs above.

## What this means for Oregana

Most of these registers are stop-list data over pipes Oregana already models; four things need new work. In Oregana a register is a rank of modelled pipes with a pitch, a scale and a voicing, so a Cornet, an Italian ripieno or a Spanish Lleno is a table, not new DSP.

| Register type | State of the pipe model on 2026-11-12 |
| --- | --- |
| Principals, open flutes, strings | Open flue pipe, by scale and voicing. Speaks in tune from 32' C to the top of the 2'. |
| Stopped flutes, Quintadena | Stopped flue pipe. |
| Chimney flutes (Rohrflöte) | Stopped pipe with a chimney, modelled as a change of bore. |
| Tapered flutes (Spitzflöte, Gemshorn) | Open pipe with a small radiating top; an approximation, no separate cone. |
| Mutations, mixtures, Cornet, Sesquialtera, ripieno | Ranks of the pipes above with pure tuning and break tables. Data only. |
| Beating stops (Unda maris, Voce umana, Voix céleste) | A rank tuned a few cents off. Data only. |
| Trumpets, Posaune, Bombarde, Clairon | Reed pipe with a conical resonator. Stable from 32' to about 2 kHz. |
| Krummhorn, Cromorne, Dulcian | Reed pipe with a half-length cylinder, odd harmonics. |
| Regal, Vox humana | The same reed with a short resonator. Not yet probed. |
| Harmonic flutes | Not modelled. Needs a double-length pipe blown to its octave. |
| Free reeds | Not modelled. A different tongue. |
| Tuba, horizontal trumpets | The same reed pipe on higher wind, or radiating straight at the listener. Not yet probed. |
| Swell box, divided stops | Not pipes: engine features, not yet written. |
| Zimbelstern, bells, drum | Not pipes. Out of scope for now. |

**The first organ.** The brief is an organ Bach would play a toccata on, so the stop list now being built is German baroque: Schnitger's plan (a chorus per division, a full pedal with 32') with the colours of Silbermann and Hildebrandt (Gamba, Cornet, Unda maris, Vox humana). 39 stops, about 2,700 pipes:

| Division | Stops |
| --- | --- |
| Hauptwerk | Principal 16', Octave 8', Viola da Gamba 8', Rohrflöte 8', Octave 4', Spitzflöte 4', Quinte 2 2/3', Superoctave 2', Cornet V, Mixtur V, Scharff III, Trompete 16', Trompete 8' |
| Positiv | Gedackt 8', Quintadena 8', Unda maris 8', Principal 4', Rohrflöte 4', Nasat 2 2/3', Octave 2', Terz 1 3/5', Quinte 1 1/3', Sifflöte 1', Cimbel III, Dulcian 16', Krummhorn 8', Vox humana 8' |
| Pedal | Untersatz 32', Principal 16', Subbass 16', Octave 8', Gedackt 8', Octave 4', Nachthorn 2', Mixtur IV, Contraposaune 32', Posaune 16', Trompete 8', Cornet 2' |
| Aids | Couplers Positiv to Hauptwerk and both manuals to Pedal, tremulant |

This list already gives the French jeu de tierce (Gedackt, Principal 4', Nasat, Octave 2', Terz) and a Cornet solo.

**The open choice.** A third, enclosed manual could carry what the other schools add: a Flûte harmonique and Flûte octaviante, a Gambe with Voix céleste, an Italian Voce umana, a French reed chorus (Basson 16', Trompette 8', Hautbois 8', Clairon 4') for the Grand jeu and for Widor, and a swell box. That makes about 51 stops and one organ that plays every repertoire, at the cost of being no single historical instrument. It needs the harmonic flute and the swell box written first.
